What moves

  • Trees: about fifty across the country bend in the wind, the crown more than the trunk. A gust crosses the map from west to east about every 13 seconds, with calmer spells between. Evergreens move less and more slowly than broadleaf trees.
  • Palms in Florida move frond by frond, each at its own pace, a little more than the inland trees.
  • Water: glints run down the river with the current, and small wave marks come and go on the ocean.
  • Boats wander, rock and pause. Each one stays inside its own patch of open water.
  • Also moving: a soft beam turns at the lighthouse, smoke rises from a few farmhouse chimneys, and the trains, steam, windmill, steamer, clouds and flags move as before.

How it stays clean

  • Moving trees are painted on their own and left out of the background, so no gap opens behind them when they move.
  • Anything painted in front of a moving tree, such as a hill crest or a nearer tree, is redrawn over it, so the tree stays behind it exactly as in the still.
  • Pause motion shows the painting at rest. It matches the approved still apart from the new trees and palms and a few extra wave marks.

Zoom

  • The map is painted at 6400 × 3600 and cut into tiles like an online map. The detail reading shows how many screen pixels each painted pixel covers. Up to 100% everything is real brushwork.
