A close-up of the Toy Town square, painted only with the map's own gouache brush and stipple. It tests whether the engine can get from "looks like SVG" toward your reference. Everything has a light source: lamps, lit windows and the shop window shade every surface, and every object casts a shadow on the cobbles.

Honest assessment

A clear step away from "SVG", not yet at the reference

Does it still look like vector shapes?Much less Every surface is now a field of brush marks with light falling across it, and the figures have shadows and faces. The edges of the buildings and awnings are still very straight, so the street reads as tidy illustration more than hand painting.
The MarinesBetter, not charming yet They have faces, uniform detail and weight on the ground, and they are not mirror images. The bodies are still built from stiff blocks, especially the straight legs and boxy coats, and the hands meet the present rather than holding it. They need softer, more rounded tailoring and a little gesture, such as one head tilted toward the other.
Light and atmosphereWorking The evening light is the biggest gain: warm pools under the lamps, glowing windows, and colour that cools away from the light. This is what makes the reference feel alive, and the engine can do it.
TextureToo even The walls use one stipple everywhere, so they read as sandpaper. The reference varies its marks: smooth stucco, visible stone blocks around the door, brick. The cobbles work, but they need clearer joints and fewer bright specks.
Detail densityBelow the reference The reference has three or four times as many small things: bow-tied garlands on every sill, a lamp at each door, poinsettias, a bench with ironwork, a nutcracker under its own lights, wreaths on the door. Much of it is now possible, since the painters exist (garland, bow, wreath, planter, lamp, present, bench). It is a matter of placing and tuning many more of them.
Next, before the characters can talk
  • Marines, round 2: rounded tailoring, bent knees and a step, heads turned to each other, fingers on the present, and a mouth that can open, which is the hook for speech.
  • Varied materials: smooth stucco with soft patches, a stone surround on the toy shop's door, real brickwork on the red house.
  • Hand-drawn edges: slightly wavering cornices, awnings that sag a little, windows that are not identical.
  • More of the reference's detail: garlands with bows on every sill, door lamps, the Christmas tree with its own lights, a few more people.
  • Then put it in the map: this becomes Toy Town's deepest zoom, fading in like today's 2× layer, with the Marines as separate animated sprites.
