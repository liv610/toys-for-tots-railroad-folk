Toys for Tots Railroad

detail —

What moves

  • Trains (red, blue and green) each run their own track from the end stations into Toy Town's station and disappear inside it. Trees and the station hall are drawn over them from a separate layer.
  • Steam puffs leave the locomotive and the paddle steamer, rise, grow and fade.
  • Windmill blades turn about the hub; the paddle wheel turns.
  • Boats bob and drift, clouds cross the sky, the six flags wave.

Zoom

  • The map is painted from code at 6400 × 3600, then cut into tiles like an online map. Only the tiles in view load.
  • The detail reading shows how many screen pixels each painted pixel covers. Up to 100% everything is real brushwork. Past that it is enlarged.
  • Every brush mark is defined in map units, so a 12800-wide render gives genuinely finer detail. That size needs the painter to work in strips to fit memory, which is the next step.

Against the reference

  • Matches: three end stations, a glass-arched hall with wreath and flags, the town row, the river under the bridge to the delta, the steamer, red and blue trains, the viaduct, the mesa, and snowy New England.
  • Short of it: the reference is denser and more golden. Its hills carry ploughed field stripes and many more small trees. Mine read as broad bands with sparser, larger trees.
  • Short of it: the reference stations and houses have more ornament: timber framing, porches, signs. Mine are simpler boxes with lit windows.
  • Short of it: the Great Lakes area is busy and unclear, and the delta has fewer channels than the reference.
